# Current product direction

## The goal

Build a continuously evolving information world model: a common evidence and identity foundation that connects people, ideas, narratives, organizations, economics, technology, decisions, historical events, and outcomes. It should explain documented relationships, investigate competing explanations, forecast observable developments, and learn through measured errors.

The architecture is broad; the first experiment and commercial product can be narrow. No information domain has universal priority. A money-trail pilot must not erase narrative analysis, professional decision-making, or portable personal and enterprise intelligence.

## What the first demonstration must prove

Acquire a selected real historical corpus; extract entities, claims, events, and nuanced typed relationships; segment information while retaining cross-domain recall; retrieve relevant evidence; generate alternatives; distinguish observation from inference; replay a past evidence state; commit and score a forecast; expose mistakes and costs; deliver the result through a simple messaging interface with an inspectable evidence trace.

Approximate but useful answers matter more than an illusion of complete accuracy. The founder particularly values seeing the system connect information to real-world outcomes and seeing measurable learning, rather than a polished chatbot demonstration alone.

## Core capabilities

- Source-preserving, time-aware memory with stable identities and reversible corrections.
- Domain-specific views over shared foundations, with cross-domain discovery.
- Bounded investigation that starts shallow and deepens when relevance or repeated evidence justifies it.
- Separate content, pathway, causal, and outcome attribution.
- Versioned professional reasoning frameworks that can be selected and tested.
- Historical replay, prospective forecasting, feedback capture, and controlled model or policy updates.
- Shared permitted intelligence, private workspaces, and explicitly authorized contributions with inherited restrictions.
- Model-independent interfaces: messaging first, then APIs, MCP, CLI, and enterprise integrations.
- Portability, expandable contracts, incremental processing, and explicit compute budgets.

## Product opportunities

Personal intelligence; market and relationship intelligence; narrative and information-integrity investigations; private enterprise decision support; intelligence infrastructure APIs. The first buyer, pricing, data licenses, and pilot ecosystem remain unvalidated.

Meeting capture and selective executive wearables are future enterprise options, not initial hardware requirements. Public research visibility can coexist with a private technical core and a small invited research group.

## What is not established

No proven proprietary moat, predictive superiority, completed implementation, validated customer commitment, selected database engine, chosen model, or confirmed capsule advantage. Existing technology and historical external claims in the transcript require fresh verification before adoption.
